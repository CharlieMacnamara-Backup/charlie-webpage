Traditional sourdough's fussy. Use a hybrid: biga + starter. Big flavour,
shorter proofs, flexible timing. Bake by feel—no scales.

Why this works:

1. Long, slow fermentation builds layered flavour
2. Parallel ferments create complementary profiles
3. Flexible timing

You'll need:

#### Ingredients

- Unrefined sea salt
- Bread flour (1.5kg bag)
- Whole-grain flour
- Active sourdough starter
- Water
- Diastatic malt powder
- Brown sugar or natural sweetener (honey, malt syrup, maple…)

#### Equipment

- Large mixing container
- Measuring cup for water
- Danish dough whisk
- Baking steel
- Shallow tray
- Sharp blade or lame

Recipe assumes 1.5kg bag of strong white bread flour (common in Scotland).

Diastatic malt boosts yeast activity.

Everything starts with biga:

Biga needs:

- 750g flour (half your bag)
- 450g water (60% hydration)
- ¼ teaspoon dry yeast
- ¼ teaspoon brown sugar or a splash of natural syrup

Dissolve the sugar and yeast in water.

### Building Strength Through Folding

Light kneading—consistency beats technique.

Stretch-and-fold: lift an edge until nearly tearing, fold to centre, rotate 90°,
repeat.

<SingleImage
  src="/blog/sourdough-journey/day2/unhydrated-flour.png"
  alt="Unhydrated flour clumps in dough"
  orientation="horizontal"
  blogSlug="sourdough-journey"
  objectFit="contain"
  aspectRatio="auto"
/>

That's what happens when you don't fold enough. Pale patches show unhydrated
flour — dry pockets that never develop structure. A couple of rounds and the
dough becomes one cohesive mass. Skip them and your crumb's dense and uneven
where those clusters sat.

After 30 minutes, switch to coil folds: slide wet hands under the centre, lift,
tuck.

### Final Shaping: Building Surface Tension

Dust bannetons with rice flour so gluten doesn't stick.

Create tension without degassing:

1. Flip a round onto a lightly floured surface
2. Fold the far edge two-thirds toward you
3. Fold the near edge over that
4. Rotate 90° and repeat
5. Flip seam-side down
6. Drag toward you to tighten
7. Rotate a quarter turn and repeat

## Day Three: The End

Dough has flavour and strength. Time to bake.

### Creating Your Baking Environment

Skip single-purpose kit. Use the open-bake method: Dutch oven results, home-oven
setup.

Set it up:

1. Baking steel on the middle rack
2. Steam pan on the bottom rack
3. Inverted tray on the top rack to trap steam
4. Heat to 250°C/480°F
5. Preheat 40 minutes for full saturation

Before baking, gather:

- Blade or lame
- Peel or board
- Heat-resistant gloves
- Spray bottle of water
- Kettle with boiling water

### Scoring

A well-placed score controls expansion and makes the ear.

<SingleImage
  src="/blog/sourdough-journey/day3/angle.png"

/>

For clean scores:

- Hold the blade at ~45°
- Make one confident ¼-inch cut
- Score right before loading
- Keep decorative cuts shallow (⅛-inch)

### The Bake

Move fast:

1. Load the loaf onto the hot steel
2. Pour boiling water into the steam pan
3. Mist the oven
4. Close the door

Two phases:

1. **Steam (30 minutes)**
   - Steam delays crust, maximizes expansion
   - Hold 250°C/480°F
2. **Crust (15–20 minutes)**
   - Remove steam pan and inverted tray (watch your hands)
   - Drop to 230°C/450°F
   - Rotate loaf 180°
   - Aim for deep caramelisation, not burnt

### The Result

You're looking for:

- Deep mahogany crust
- A pronounced ear
- Hollow sound when tapped underneath

Don't slice warm. Let crumb set and moisture equalize. Crust and flavour improve
as it cools.

### Keep It Simple

This method will evolve, but the principles don't: patient fermentation, gentle
handling, attentive baking. Tweak as you go—hydration, flour, seeds. There's no
single right way; bread's oddly personal.

Less fuss, better bread.
